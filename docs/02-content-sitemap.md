# Sitemap & Content

## Site structure

A single-page scroll site is tempting for "simple," but this content (problem,
solution, market, team, ask) is too long to be one scroll without losing
people. Use a small multi-page structure instead, with a short, scannable
home page that links out:

```
/               Home
/about          About Us (problem + solution + why now)
/how-it-works   How It Works (rider flow, app, charging, safety)
/impact         Impact & Sustainability
/team           Team
/get-involved   Get Involved (the ask: funders, partners, universities)
/contact        Contact
```

If a single page is preferred instead, keep this same section order as
anchor sections (`/#about`, `/#impact`, etc.) rather than redesigning the
flow — the order below is deliberate (problem before solution, solution
before market, market before ask).

Nav: Home / About / How It Works / Impact / Team / Get Involved, with
Contact as a persistent header button rather than a nav item (it's the
conversion point — keep it visually distinct from the informational pages).

## About Us

This page carries the actual case, condensed from the pitch document —
write it as prose, not bullet soup, since this is the page people read when
deciding whether to take the project seriously.

**The problem**
Nigerian university campuses are large, and current transport options
(walking long distances, or relying on crowded fuel-powered shuttles) cost
students time, money, and safety. Fuel subsidy removal has pushed transport
costs up further. Most campus vehicles run on petrol or diesel, and there's
little visible alternative.

**The solution**
A campus-based electric scooter-sharing service: a fleet of e-scooters,
solar-powered charging stations, and a simple app to unlock, ride, and pay.
Geofenced to campus for safety. Priced for student budgets, not commercial
rates.

**Why Malmö + Nigeria**
Malmö is one of Europe's most bicycle- and micromobility-fluent cities —
that operational knowledge (charging infrastructure, fleet management, safe
geofencing) transfers directly. The Nigerian market is large, young, and
already primed: Lagos State University ran a successful e-scooter pilot in
2023, and national policy (the EV Transition Bill, TETFUND's own electric
shuttle pilot) is moving the same direction. ScandAfriq sits at the
intersection of both.

**Why now**
Link the three forces from the plan: rising fuel costs making electric
options financially attractive, a young population already comfortable
with app-based services (ride-hailing is already mainstream in Nigerian
cities), and early local competitors (Trekk, Helgg) proving demand exists
without yet covering the market.

## How It Works

Written for a prospective student user and a prospective university partner
reading the same page — keep both groups in mind in the copy.

1. **Find a scooter** — open the app, see nearby scooters on a campus map.
2. **Unlock and ride** — scan a QR code, ride anywhere within the
   geofenced campus perimeter.
3. **Park and pay** — end the ride at an approved spot, pay automatically
   at a student-friendly rate.
4. **Charge, sustainably** — scooters return to solar-powered charging
   stations, not the grid.

Safety callout (separate visual block, not buried in the steps): helmets
provided, in-app safety tutorial required before first ride, geofenced to
campus only, campus security and administration partnership.

University partner callout: what the university provides (campus access,
charging station space, official endorsement) vs. what ScandAfriq provides
(fleet, app, local ops team, maintenance) — this is the section university
administrators will actually read, give it real visual weight, not an
afterthought paragraph.

## Impact & Sustainability

Lead with the numbers, since this is the page grant reviewers will scan
first:

- Estimated CO2 saved per replaced car/shuttle trip
- Time saved per student per day (walking vs. scooter)
- Cost saved per student per term vs. existing transport costs
- Local jobs created per campus deployment (student ops roles, maintenance)

Frame these explicitly against UN SDGs already named in the plan: Climate
Action (13), Reduced Inequalities (10), Sustainable Cities (11), Decent
Work (8) — short one-line each, not a logo wall.

Close with the "living lab" framing: each campus becomes a visible proof
point for solar-powered transport, not just a service.

## Get Involved

This is the conversion page for the three real audiences from the plan —
structure it as three distinct blocks, not one generic "contact us":

1. **Funders & grant bodies** — what's being sought (seed funding for pilot
   hardware + charging infrastructure), and a short note that impact data
   will be reported transparently (annual impact report commitment from
   the plan).
2. **University partners** — invitation to pilot, what the university gets,
   link back to the partner callout on `/how-it-works`.
3. **Mobility/energy partners & mentors** — solar/energy companies, telecom,
   accelerator and incubator connections (Malmö + Lagos hubs named in the
   plan).

Each block ends in a short form or mailto link, not all funneling into one
generic form — different audiences, different first message.

## Contact

Keep minimal: a short form (name, email, which of the three audiences above,
message) plus a direct email address. No live chat widget needed at this
stage — it implies a team that's staffed for it.
